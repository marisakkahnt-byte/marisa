Hi, friend!!

Welcome to my brain! I'm Marisa Kahnt (pronounced "can't," which is ironic, because I'm a big believer in "how hard can it be"). It's a little crowded in here. By day I'm on the talent operations and strategy side of the house at Block, keeping everything running smoothly so we find and hire the best people (and they actually start!). I'd rather eliminate a problem than build a fix for it.

I was born with that gene, so by night (and most weekends) I'm a serial hobbyist who took it too far. I run my Brother embroidery machine at Marisa Stitches (busy hands, quiet mind), and because I love a good love story almost as much as I love telling one, I write and officiate ceremonies (and coach vows!) with I Do by Marisa.

I'm learning sourdough, getting into genealogy, I'd love to write a children's book one day, and I love to travel, which is where my type A finally turns into type B. I love all things reality TV, and fully expect to end up on a competition show one day. The hobbies will not stop. I have accepted this.

Every building on this street is a different corner of my brain. Pick a door. I promise there are no extra steps.

How I work

  • CuriousFirst question is always "why do we do it this way?" Second is usually "can we not?"
  • Born with the geneThe "how hard can it be" gene, specifically. If I don't know how to do something yet, that's just Tuesday. I ramp fast and figure it out.
  • Leads with empathyPeople are usually doing their best with a broken process. I assume good intent first, always.
  • "Yes, and"I treat work like improv: always looking for the "yes, and," not the reason it won't work. Failing just means I learned something before you had to.
  • Allergic to extra stepsIf a process has more than five steps, I'm already redesigning it in my head. I promise I'm listening too.
  • Knows the roomI can tell you exactly who needs to be looped in, who doesn't, and who just wants the recap after.
  • Doesn't gatekeepI'd rather teach you the fix than be the only one who can do it. When one of us shines, we all shine.
  • CraftI sweat the details nobody asked for. The last 10% is the part people remember.
