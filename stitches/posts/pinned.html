@marisastitches

needlepoint

stitched on
stiff canvas

every hole gets filled, so the whole
canvas turns into thread. usually
wool or silk, often hand-painted.

@marisastitches

cross stitch

little x’s
on a grid

counted fabric (aida), tiny x’s,
a chart you follow square by square.
pixel art, but make it thread.

@marisastitches

embroidery

anything goes,
any fabric

by hand: freestyle stitches on fabric
in a hoop.

by machine: a design digitized and
stitched out on hats, towels, totes.
(this is the one i take orders for,
when the shop is open)

needlepoint for beginners

5 things i
wish i knew

save this for your first canvas →

@marisastitches
  1. start with a painted canvasthe design is right there, you just fill it in.
  2. use stretcher barskeeps the canvas tight and square.
  3. blunt tapestry needleit slides through holes, not thread.
@marisastitches
  1. keep your thread shortabout 18 inches, so it doesn’t fray.
  2. basketweave for big areasit warps the canvas less than
    row-by-row stitching.

bonus: turning it into an ornament or
pillow (“finishing”) is its own step.
